The Condition Reality — Why This Drives Everything

The Single Biggest Factor

Carpet-Heavy in a 2026 Market Means You Cannot Compete As-Is

The Caramella property has almost exclusively carpet flooring with very little hard floor surface. In the 2026 buyer and tenant market, this is the #1 fixable condition issue and it affects both paths equally:

If You Try to Sell As-Is

  • Buyers compare your property to competing inventory with luxury vinyl plank, tile, or wood — and discount aggressively for the difference
  • Inspection negotiations target carpet condition, age, allergens, and pet history
  • Realistic pricing as-is: $325,000–$340,000 versus $345,000–$365,000 with refresh — a $15,000–$25,000 gap
  • Days on market extend significantly — comparable Tivoli Villa Dr (2005, similar profile) has been listed 162 days unsold at $336,900

If You Try to Re-Rent As-Is

  • Tenant pool narrows — quality renters in 2026 expect hard floor surfaces in main living areas
  • Rent ceiling: $2,200–$2,300/mo (same as previous tenant or slight uplift)
  • Longer vacancy time as you wait for the right tenant willing to accept the carpet
  • Tenant retention drops — even good tenants leave when newer alternatives become available

The Refresh Investment Pays Back Either Way

  • Luxury vinyl plank in main living areas + bedrooms: ~$5,500–$8,000 for 1,779 sqft
  • Full interior repaint (neutral palette): ~$2,500–$3,500
  • Minor updates (light fixtures, hardware, deep clean): ~$1,000–$1,500
  • Total realistic refresh budget: $9,000–$13,000
  • Adds ~$15,000–$25,000 to sale price OR ~$200–$300/mo to rent — paying back in either path

The Tax Reality — Why Selling Has a Heavier Cost for You Than Most Owners

⚠ This conversation matters more for your property than for most

You purchased this property in 2009 for $119,900, which means you have 17 years of appreciation sitting in this asset. That is a meaningful unrealized gain — and selling triggers federal tax exposure that is significantly larger than typical newer-purchase scenarios.

The Honest Comparison Math

If you take Path A and rent for 5 years before selling, you collect approximately $144,000 in gross rental income while also likely benefiting from continued appreciation. Even after subtracting vacancy, maintenance, and management costs, you net considerably more income than the marginal increase you might capture by selling now versus 5 years from now — and you preserve the $42K–$50K tax exposure for a future strategy (potentially via 1031 exchange).

The only scenarios where Path B (sell now) wins are:

  • You have a specific need for the lump-sum capital — relocation, debt payoff, family obligation, opportunity elsewhere
  • You are simplifying your investment portfolio and want to exit landlord obligations
  • Your CPA has identified a specific tax-year reason to take the gain now (rare)
  • You believe the 32829 market will deteriorate further over the next 24 months (the data does not support this)

If none of those apply to you, the numbers favor refresh and re-rent strongly.
